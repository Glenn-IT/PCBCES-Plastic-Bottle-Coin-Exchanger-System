Safety Checklist & Safe Power-Up Sequence
⚠️

Do NOT Power SIM800L or MG996R from Arduino 5V Pin!

The Arduino Uno onboard 5V regulator can only output ~400mA. The MG996R servo draws up to 1.5A peak and the SIM800L draws 2A bursts during transmission. Always power them from the LM2596 5V rail backed by the 1000µF capacitor!

⚡

Voltage Divider Verification Required

Before connecting D5, D6, and D7 to the Arduino, measure the signal wire with your multimeter. With a 10kΩ and 4.7kΩ divider, the voltage must read between 3.6V and 4.0V (safe for Arduino). Never feed raw 12V into an Arduino pin.

  1. Step 1: Tune LM2596 Output Voltage

    Connect S-120-12 PSU to AC mains. Turn the brass potentiometer screw on the LM2596 until your multimeter reads exactly 5.00V DC across OUT+ and OUT-.

  2. Step 2: Establish Central Star Ground

    Tie PSU (V-), Buck (OUT-), GSM GND, and Arduino GND into one solid common breadboard rail. This eliminates floating ground noise.

  3. Step 3: Wire Resistor Dividers for 12V Signals

    Build two identical 10kΩ/4.7kΩ voltage divider pairs on the breadboard for the LJ12A3 Inductive metal sensor and the Coin Hopper pulse wire. (LJC18A3 is omitted; Pin D5 is spare).

  4. Step 4: Connect I2C LCD & Calibrate Contrast

    Wire SDA to A4 and SCL to A5. Adjust the small blue potentiometer on the back of the I2C backpack until clear white characters appear on the blue screen.

  5. Step 5: Test GSM SMS Dispatch

    Insert active SIM card. Verify that the SIM module netlight transitions from fast blinking (1/s) to slow blinking (every 3s), indicating network registration.
